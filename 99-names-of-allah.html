99 Names of Allah with Meaning (Asma ul Husna)

Explore the commonly listed Asma ul Husna in Arabic, transliteration and English meaning. This reference page is designed for reading and learning, not as a claim that every scholar uses the same enumeration.

How to learn the 99 Names of Allah

Many people study the Names by reading a few at a time, learning their meanings and reflecting on how those meanings relate to worship and supplication. Transliteration can vary between books and regions, and an English gloss is only a brief aid; it cannot capture every nuance of the Arabic name.

The hadith about the ninety-nine Names and their enumeration is reported in Sahih al-Bukhari and Sahih Muslim. Scholars have discussed the exact enumeration, so this page presents one widely used list rather than suggesting that there is no scholarly difference.

Using a counter while studying

You can select a short dhikr from the counter or add a personal entry to track repetition while you study. Do not assume that every Name has a prescribed repetition count; follow a reliable source or qualified teacher for any specific devotional practice.

Frequently asked questions

What does Asma ul Husna mean?

Asma ul Husna means the Most Beautiful Names. It is a common expression for the Names of Allah.

Are these all the meanings of the 99 Names?

The English descriptions are short glosses. Arabic meanings can carry nuance, and translations may differ across reliable references.

Do I need to repeat each Name 33 or 100 times?

This page does not prescribe a count for each Name. Use a repeated count only when it is supported by a reliable source or a personal study plan.
