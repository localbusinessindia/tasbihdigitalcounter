Salawat and Durood: Meaning and Ibrahimiyyah

A source-led introduction to sending blessings upon Prophet Muhammad ﷺ, including the well-known Salat Ibrahimiyyah wording.

اللَّهُمَّ صَلِّ عَلَى مُحَمَّدٍ

Salawat (also called durood in South Asian usage) refers to asking Allah to send blessings upon Prophet Muhammad ﷺ.

Why do Muslims send salawat?

The Qur’an instructs believers to send blessings and greetings upon the Prophet ﷺ in Surah al-Ahzab 33:56. Salawat is recited in salah and may also be said outside prayer.

Salat Ibrahimiyyah

A well-known wording is the Salat Ibrahimiyyah, which begins Allahumma salli ‘ala Muhammadin wa ‘ala ali Muhammad… The Prophet ﷺ taught a form of this wording when companions asked how to send blessings upon him. Read Sahih al-Bukhari 3370 for the narration and full wording.

Opening transliteration: Allahumma salli ‘ala Muhammadin wa ‘ala ali Muhammad.

This is only the opening portion, not the complete Salat Ibrahimiyyah. Use the linked hadith or a reliable prayer text for the full wording.

Is there a fixed number of durood?

This page does not assign a universal daily count. If you follow a specific devotional routine taught by a scholar or source, check its evidence and context. You can also send salawat without turning a personal counter target into a religious requirement.

Using a counter for salawat

A digital counter can help you keep track of a personal routine. Choose a number only as a planning aid, and make sure you recite the full wording you intend to say.

What is salawat and durood?

Salawat refers to invoking Allah's blessings upon the Prophet Muhammad (peace and blessings be upon him). Durood is a familiar South Asian term used for sending blessings upon the Prophet. Muslims recite salawat in salah and may also send blessings at other times. The wording can be short or part of a longer supplication, so learn each form from a reliable source.

Salawat Ibrahimiyyah

The Ibrahimiyyah wording is a well-known form recited in the sitting portion of salah. It asks Allah to send blessings upon Muhammad and his family, as He sent blessings upon Ibrahim and his family. Because this is a longer text, use a trusted Arabic source with a checked translation and do not rely on a shortened transliteration if you are learning it for prayer.

How often should I read durood?

Muslims are encouraged to send blessings upon the Prophet, but specific counts should be tied to reliable evidence. If a book or website recommends a personal daily target, distinguish that goal from a count established by a particular narration. A digital counter can help track a chosen amount, but it cannot determine the religious status of that target.

Arabic, transliteration and spelling variants

Searches may use salawat, salawaat, durood, darood, durood shareef or durood sharif. These spellings often point to the same broad topic, although individual supplications may have different wording. Check the exact Arabic text before reciting a particular version.
