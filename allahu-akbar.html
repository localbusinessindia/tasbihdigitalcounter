Allahu Akbar Meaning in Arabic and English

A clear guide to the phrase Allahu Akbar, its meaning, pronunciation and examples from reported remembrance.

اللَّٰهُ أَكْبَرُ

Allahu Akbar is commonly translated as “Allah is the Greatest” or “Allah is greater.” It is the phrase of takbir, declaring Allah’s greatness.

Count Allahu Akbar

When is Allahu Akbar said?

Takbir is part of the adhan, salah and many forms of Islamic remembrance. Muslims also say Allahu Akbar when expressing awe of Allah’s greatness or responding to significant moments. The exact context and wording matter when following a prescribed act of worship.

Allahu Akbar in bedtime remembrance

One reported bedtime version taught to Ali and Fatimah includes Allahu Akbar 34 times, SubhanAllah 33 times and Alhamdulillah 33 times. See Sahih Muslim 2727a and the related report in Sahih al-Bukhari 5362. Related narrations present wording and count details differently; read the bedtime dhikr guide for the distinction.

Takbir after salah

In one post-prayer remembrance sequence, Allahu Akbar is said 34 times after 33 repetitions each of SubhanAllah and Alhamdulillah. See Sahih Muslim 596a. Do not assume every dhikr routine uses the same count or order.

Allahu Akbar meaning and significance

Allahu Akbar means “Allah is the Greatest.” It is the takbir: a declaration of Allah's greatness. Muslims hear and say this phrase in the call to prayer, during salah, on significant occasions and in daily remembrance. Its meaning reminds a believer that Allah is greater than every concern, possession or worldly power.

Takbir, tasbih and tahmid

Allahu Akbar is takbir; SubhanAllah is tasbih; and Alhamdulillah is tahmid. These phrases are sometimes grouped in dhikr after prayer and before sleep. Although the same words may appear in both settings, the number and sequence can differ between narrations. Follow the source for the specific routine you are learning.

How many times is Allahu Akbar said in bedtime dhikr?

Some bedtime narrations describe Allahu Akbar 34 times alongside 33 repetitions each of SubhanAllah and Alhamdulillah. Other reports present the same 100-count remembrance with a different order. Read the bedtime guide and its cited references for the exact wording rather than assuming one sequence is the only narration.

Allahu Akbar in Arabic and transliteration

The Arabic is اللَّهُ أَكْبَرُ. Common English spellings include Allahu Akbar and Allah-o-Akbar. Transliteration helps readers approximate the sound, but it is not a substitute for learning the Arabic pronunciation from a reliable teacher or recitation.
