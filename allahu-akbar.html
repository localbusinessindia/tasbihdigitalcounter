Allahu Akbar Meaning in Arabic and English

A clear guide to the phrase Allahu Akbar, its meaning, pronunciation and examples from reported remembrance.

اللَّٰهُ أَكْبَرُ

Allahu Akbar is commonly translated as “Allah is the Greatest” or “Allah is greater.” It is the phrase of takbir, declaring Allah’s greatness.

Count Allahu Akbar

When is Allahu Akbar said?

Takbir is part of the adhan, salah and many forms of Islamic remembrance. Muslims also say Allahu Akbar when expressing awe of Allah’s greatness or responding to significant moments. The exact context and wording matter when following a prescribed act of worship.

Allahu Akbar in bedtime remembrance

One reported bedtime version taught to Ali and Fatimah includes Allahu Akbar 34 times, SubhanAllah 33 times and Alhamdulillah 33 times. See Sahih Muslim 2727a and the related report in Sahih al-Bukhari 5362. Related narrations present wording and count details differently; read the bedtime dhikr guide for the distinction.

Takbir after salah

In one post-prayer remembrance sequence, Allahu Akbar is said 34 times after 33 repetitions each of SubhanAllah and Alhamdulillah. See Sahih Muslim 596a. Do not assume every dhikr routine uses the same count or order.

Counting Allahu Akbar

Choose the relevant guided routine or set a personal goal in the counter. If you are following a narrated practice, use its cited wording and sequence rather than mixing counts from different reports.

Frequently asked questions

What does Allahu Akbar mean?

It means “Allah is the Greatest” or “Allah is greater.”

How do you write Allahu Akbar in Arabic?

اللَّٰهُ أَكْبَرُ

How many times is Allahu Akbar said before sleep?

One reported bedtime sequence includes 34 repetitions; related narrations have variant wording. See Sahih Muslim 2727a and Bukhari 5362.

Is Allahu Akbar only said in prayer?

No. It appears in prayer and in other forms of remembrance, with context-specific wording and counts.
