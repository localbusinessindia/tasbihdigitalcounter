La Ilaha Illallah Meaning in Arabic and English

Understand the declaration of faith La ilaha illallah, its meaning and the difference between the short phrase and longer narrated formulas.

لَا إِلَٰهَ إِلَّا اللَّٰهُ

La ilaha illallah means “There is no deity worthy of worship except Allah.” It is a central declaration of Islamic belief and is known as tahlil.

Count La ilaha illallah

Meaning and transliteration

The phrase is commonly written as La ilaha illallah, Laa ilaaha illallaah or La ilaha illa Allah. Transliteration varies, but the Arabic phrase is the same. Its meaning affirms worship belongs to Allah alone.

A longer form in hadith

A narration in Sahih Muslim 2691 mentions a longer remembrance beginning “La ilaha illallah wahdahu la sharika lah…” and describes its virtue when said 100 times in a day. That report concerns the complete wording, not only the short phrase on this page. Read the linked narration for the full text and conditions.

Use the phrase thoughtfully

La ilaha illallah is a statement of faith, not merely a counting exercise. A counter may help you keep track of repetitions, but use the complete wording when following a specific narration and give attention to what the declaration means.

Frequently asked questions

What does La ilaha illallah mean?

It means “There is no deity worthy of worship except Allah.”

How is La ilaha illallah written in Arabic?

لَا إِلَٰهَ إِلَّا اللَّٰهُ

What is tahlil?

Tahlil is the remembrance or declaration “La ilaha illallah.”
