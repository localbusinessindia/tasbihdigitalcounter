La Ilaha Illallah Meaning in Arabic and English

Understand the declaration of faith La ilaha illallah, its meaning and the difference between the short phrase and longer narrated formulas.

لَا إِلَٰهَ إِلَّا اللَّٰهُ

La ilaha illallah means “There is no deity worthy of worship except Allah.” It is a central declaration of Islamic belief and is known as tahlil.

Meaning and transliteration

The phrase is commonly written as La ilaha illallah, Laa ilaaha illallaah or La ilaha illa Allah. Transliteration varies, but the Arabic phrase is the same. Its meaning affirms worship belongs to Allah alone.

A longer form in hadith

A narration in Sahih Muslim 2691 mentions a longer remembrance beginning “La ilaha illallah wahdahu la sharika lah…” and describes its virtue when said 100 times in a day. That report concerns the complete wording, not only the short phrase on this page. Read the linked narration for the full text and conditions.

Use the phrase thoughtfully

La ilaha illallah is a statement of faith, not merely a counting exercise. A counter may help you keep track of repetitions, but use the complete wording when following a specific narration and give attention to what the declaration means.

La ilaha illallah meaning in Islam

La ilaha illallah is the declaration that there is no deity worthy of worship except Allah. It is the central statement of tawhid (the oneness of Allah) and a foundational phrase in Islam. A translation should communicate both the negation of worship directed to anything else and the affirmation that worship belongs to Allah alone.

La ilaha illallah in daily dhikr

Muslims say this declaration in prayer, in remembrance and in many other contexts. Some narrations mention particular counts or forms of tahlil, but a general daily repetition should not automatically be assigned a fixed Sunnah number. Check the wording and source when a specific count is quoted.

Difference between tahlil, tasbih and tahmid

Tahlil refers to saying La ilaha illallah. Tasbih is saying SubhanAllah, and tahmid is saying Alhamdulillah. Takbir is saying Allahu Akbar. These are related forms of dhikr but they do not have identical meanings. Learning the difference makes it easier to understand a narrated routine and the sequence in which its phrases are recited.

Arabic text and transliteration

The Arabic wording is لَا إِلَٰهَ إِلَّا اللَّهُ. It is commonly transliterated as La ilaha illallah, Laa ilaaha illallah or La ilaha illa Allah. Transliteration conventions vary, so use the Arabic text and a reliable recitation to learn the pronunciation accurately.
