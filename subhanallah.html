سُبْحَانَ اللَّٰهِ

SubhanAllah means “Glory be to Allah.” It declares that Allah is free from imperfection, weakness and anything that does not befit His majesty.

What does SubhanAllah mean?

SubhanAllah is a form of tasbih and a common expression of dhikr. A Muslim may say it while reflecting on creation, after salah or during the day. It is often translated as “Glory be to Allah,” although the Arabic carries the deeper sense of declaring Allah far above every imperfection.

SubhanAllahi wa bihamdihi

سُبْحَانَ اللَّٰهِ وَبِحَمْدِهِ
SubhanAllahi wa bihamdihi

Meaning: “Glory and praise be to Allah.”

Sahih al-Bukhari 6405 reports that whoever says SubhanAllahi wa bihamdihi one hundred times in a day will have their sins forgiven, even if they are like the foam of the sea. The wording of this narration refers to the combined phrase above, not simply to any phrase that contains the word SubhanAllah. Read Sahih al-Bukhari 6405.

Two phrases that are light on the tongue

Another narration in Sahih al-Bukhari 6406 mentions two expressions that are easy to say, heavy in the scale and beloved to the Most Merciful: SubhanAllahi wa bihamdihi and SubhanAllahil-Azeem. This is a reminder to keep remembrance sincere and consistent.

How many times do you say SubhanAllah?

The count depends on the dhikr being followed. In one authentic post-salah narration, SubhanAllah is said 33 times, followed by Alhamdulillah 33 times and Allahu Akbar 34 times (Sahih Muslim 596a). The combined phrase SubhanAllahi wa bihamdihi has a separate narration mentioning 100 repetitions in a day. Keep these practices distinct when choosing a goal.

Frequently asked questions

What is SubhanAllah in English?

SubhanAllah is commonly translated as “Glory be to Allah.”

Is SubhanAllahi wa bihamdihi different from SubhanAllah?

Yes. It adds “wa bihamdihi” (“and with His praise”), making the phrase “Glory and praise be to Allah.” Specific narrations mention this full phrase with a count of 100.

Can I say SubhanAllah at any time?

It is a common form of remembrance that may be said throughout the day. For prescribed routines and counts, follow the wording and context of the relevant source.
