What does SubhanAllah mean?

SubhanAllah is a form of tasbih and a common expression of dhikr. A Muslim may say it while reflecting on creation, after salah or during the day. It is often translated as “Glory be to Allah,” although the Arabic carries the deeper sense of declaring Allah far above every imperfection.

SubhanAllahi wa bihamdihi

سُبْحَانَ اللَّٰهِ وَبِحَمْدِهِ
SubhanAllahi wa bihamdihi

Meaning: “Glory and praise be to Allah.”

Sahih al-Bukhari 6405 reports that whoever says SubhanAllahi wa bihamdihi one hundred times in a day will have their sins forgiven, even if they are like the foam of the sea. The wording of this narration refers to the combined phrase above, not simply to any phrase that contains the word SubhanAllah. Read Sahih al-Bukhari 6405.

Two phrases that are light on the tongue

Another narration in Sahih al-Bukhari 6406 mentions two expressions that are easy to say, heavy in the scale and beloved to the Most Merciful: SubhanAllahi wa bihamdihi and SubhanAllahil-Azeem. This is a reminder to keep remembrance sincere and consistent.

How many times do you say SubhanAllah?

The count depends on the dhikr being followed. In one authentic post-salah narration, SubhanAllah is said 33 times, followed by Alhamdulillah 33 times and Allahu Akbar 34 times (Sahih Muslim 596a). The combined phrase SubhanAllahi wa bihamdihi has a separate narration mentioning 100 repetitions in a day. Keep these practices distinct when choosing a goal.

SubhanAllah meaning and when to say it

SubhanAllah is a phrase of tasbih: it declares Allah free from imperfection and expresses His glory. Muslims may say it while reflecting on creation, during everyday remembrance, and within specific forms of dhikr taught in the Sunnah. The context matters: a general remembrance phrase should not be confused with a prescribed prayer formula that has a particular count or time.

SubhanAllah, Alhamdulillah and Allahu Akbar

These three phrases are often mentioned together but have distinct meanings. SubhanAllah glorifies Allah; Alhamdulillah praises and thanks Allah; Allahu Akbar declares that Allah is greater than everything. In some post-prayer and bedtime narrations they are recited in a particular sequence. Follow the cited narration for the exact order and number rather than combining versions from memory.

SubhanAllahi wa bihamdihi meaning

The longer phrase SubhanAllahi wa bihamdihi is commonly translated as “Glory be to Allah and praise is His” or “Glory and praise be to Allah.” It combines glorification with praise. It is distinct from saying SubhanAllah alone, so when reading a hadith about a particular phrase, make sure the full Arabic wording matches the translation and count being discussed.

Can SubhanAllah be said 33 or 100 times?

Different narrations describe different remembrances and counts in different contexts. A count of 33 appears in well-known post-prayer and bedtime routines, while other reports mention SubhanAllahi wa bihamdihi 100 times in a day. These are not interchangeable instructions: check which exact phrase and context a source is describing. A personal counter goal can help with tracking, but it should not be presented as a prescribed Sunnah count unless a reliable source supports it.

Pronunciation and transliteration

SubhanAllah is also written Subhan Allah, SubhaanAllah or Subhanallah. Transliteration styles differ, especially when long vowels are represented. Use the Arabic text سُبْحَانَ اللَّهِ as a reference and learn pronunciation from a trusted teacher or reciter if you are unsure.

Frequently asked questions

What is SubhanAllah in English?

SubhanAllah is commonly translated as “Glory be to Allah.”

Is SubhanAllahi wa bihamdihi different from SubhanAllah?

Yes. It adds “wa bihamdihi” (“and with His praise”), making the phrase “Glory and praise be to Allah.” Specific narrations mention this full phrase with a count of 100.

Can I say SubhanAllah at any time?

It is a common form of remembrance that may be said throughout the day. For prescribed routines and counts, follow the wording and context of the relevant source.
