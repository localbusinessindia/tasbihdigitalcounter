Alhamdulillah Meaning in Arabic and English

When do people say Alhamdulillah?

Muslims say Alhamdulillah when expressing gratitude, after receiving a blessing, when acknowledging something good, and in many forms of worship. Its meaning is broader than a casual “thank you”: it directs praise to Allah.

Alhamdulillah in post-salah dhikr

One well-known post-prayer sequence includes saying SubhanAllah 33 times, Alhamdulillah 33 times and Allahu Akbar 34 times. See Sahih Muslim 596a. Other narrations describe related forms of remembrance, so follow the wording of the source you are using.

Alhamdulillah and SubhanAllahi wa bihamdihi

Alhamdulillah is a standalone phrase of praise. SubhanAllahi wa bihamdihi is a different, combined phrase meaning “Glory and praise be to Allah.” The morning-and-evening narration for repeating the latter 100 times is in Sahih Muslim 2692.

Alhamdulillah meaning: praise and gratitude

Alhamdulillah is a phrase of praise and gratitude to Allah. It is often translated as “All praise is for Allah” or “Praise be to Allah.” Muslims say it in everyday conversation, in prayer and in remembrance. The phrase recognises that praise belongs to Allah and encourages a person to notice blessings with humility and thankfulness.

When do Muslims say Alhamdulillah?

It is commonly said when expressing gratitude for a blessing, after sneezing, when responding that one is well, and in many forms of worship. The appropriate wording can depend on the situation and the relevant teaching. For a specific Sunnah response or supplication, check a reliable hadith source rather than assuming every everyday use has a prescribed count.

Alhamdulillah with SubhanAllah and Allahu Akbar

Alhamdulillah is tahmid (praise), SubhanAllah is tasbih (glorification), and Allahu Akbar is takbir (declaring Allah's greatness). These phrases are often recited together in reported dhikr routines. Their order and number depend on the narration being followed, so use the relevant guide for after-salah remembrance or bedtime dhikr.

Arabic spelling and pronunciation

The Arabic wording is الْحَمْدُ لِلَّهِ. English transliterations include Alhamdulillah, Al-hamdu lillah and Alhamdu lillah. Spellings vary, but the Arabic text remains the reference for learning the phrase. Listen to a trusted reciter or teacher if you want help with pronunciation.

Frequently asked questions

What does Alhamdulillah mean?

It means “All praise is for Allah” or “Praise be to Allah.”

How do you write Alhamdulillah in Arabic?

الْحَمْدُ لِلَّٰهِ

How many times is Alhamdulillah said after salah?

One widely cited narration gives 33 repetitions in a post-prayer sequence; see Sahih Muslim 596a.

Is Alhamdulillah the same as SubhanAllah?

No. Alhamdulillah expresses praise and gratitude, while SubhanAllah declares Allah free from imperfection.
