Digital Tasbih for Beginners

A plain, step-by-step introduction to counting dhikr on a phone or computer, with advice on targets, accuracy and staying focused.

Open the free tasbih counter

What a digital tasbih does

A digital tasbih replaces the beads or finger joints you would otherwise use to keep count. You tap once for each recitation and the screen shows your total, your target and how much is left. It does not change what you recite or how: it only keeps the number for you so that your attention can stay on the words.

Step 1: choose one phrase

Start with a single phrase that you already know how to say correctly, such as SubhanAllah, Alhamdulillah, Allahu Akbar or Astaghfirullah. The counter’s dhikr list shows the Arabic, transliteration and meaning of 28 phrases, and each one opens the counter with that phrase selected. If you are unsure about pronunciation, ask a teacher or use a verified recording.

Step 2: decide on a target

A target is simply the number you plan to say. Some routines have a narrated number, such as 33-33-34 after prayer; read the after-salah guide for the sources. For other phrases, a target is a personal choice, so pick a number you can say with attention. Choose “no limit” if you would rather stop by time than by count.

Step 3: count with attention

  1. Say the phrase, then tap once. Do not tap first and recite later.
  2. If you tap by mistake, use Undo. It removes the last tap, so your total stays honest.
  3. If you lose your place, it is fine to stop and begin the set again.
  4. Turn on Sound or Vibration if you want a signal when you reach the target, so you do not need to watch the screen.

Step 4: save and review

Your counts, daily total, streak and best day are saved in your browser on your device. Nothing is uploaded. Because browsers can clear their data, use Export backup now and then. A daily goal and a streak are tools for staying consistent; they are not a measure of sincerity.

Common beginner mistakes

  • Setting a target that is too large. A smaller set you finish with attention is better than a large one you rush.
  • Treating a personal target as a rule. Keep narrated counts and personal goals separate in your mind.
  • Counting while distracted. Try Focus mode, which hides everything except the counter.
  • Forgetting the meaning. Read the translation once so that you know what you are saying.

Keep your count safe when changing devices

The counter saves information in the browser storage of the device you are using. That is convenient, but it is not the same as an online account: your progress does not automatically follow you to another phone or computer. Before changing devices, open the counter settings and export a backup. Keep the downloaded file somewhere you control, then use Import on the new device. Do not assume a browser sync or phone backup will include this site's local data.

If you clear browser data, use private browsing, uninstall a browser or reset a device, locally saved counts may be lost. A backup is the safest way to keep a copy. Test the export once while you still have the original device, rather than waiting until after you have changed phones.

When a sound or vibration does not happen

Sound and vibration depend on the browser, device settings and hardware. Check that the matching setting is enabled, that your phone is not silenced, and that the browser is allowed to play sound. Vibration is not supported by every device or browser, and a page cannot force unsupported hardware to vibrate. Keep the on-screen count as the source of truth; an alert is only a reminder.

Where to go next

When you are comfortable, try the step-by-step after-salah routine, read about digital and physical tasbih, or learn how to build a daily dhikr habit. You can also add the counter to your home screen so it opens like an app.

Frequently asked questions

Is a digital tasbih as good as beads?

It is a counting tool, just as beads are. Many people use whichever helps them keep their attention on the words. For a ruling on which method is preferable, ask a scholar you trust.

Do I need an account?

No. The counter works without sign-up, and your progress stays in your browser.

Does it work without internet?

After your first visit the pages are cached by your browser, so the counter can keep working offline on supported browsers.
