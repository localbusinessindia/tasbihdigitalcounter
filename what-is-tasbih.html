What Is a Tasbih? Meaning of Tasbih and Tasbeeh

Learn what tasbih means, how tasbeeh is used in everyday language, and how tasbih relates to dhikr and Islamic remembrance.

Tasbih meaning

Tasbih (also spelled tasbeeh) means glorifying Allah and declaring Him free from every imperfection. A familiar phrase of tasbih is SubhanAllah, commonly translated as “Glory be to Allah.” Tasbih is an important form of dhikr, or remembrance of Allah.

What is the difference between tasbih and dhikr?

Dhikr is a broad term for remembering Allah through words, supplications and acts of worship. Tasbih is one type of dhikr that glorifies Allah. Other common forms include tahmid (praising Allah with Alhamdulillah), takbir (declaring Allah’s greatness with Allahu Akbar) and tahlil (saying La ilaha illallah).

What are tasbih or tasbeeh beads?

In everyday English, people also use “tasbih” or “tasbeeh” to refer to prayer beads used to keep count of repeated dhikr. A digital tasbih counter performs the counting function on a phone or computer. You can also count on your fingers; prayer beads or a digital tool can help with counting, while the dhikr remains the focus.

Tasbih, tasbeeh and spelling variations

You may see the same word written as tasbih, tasbeeh, tesbih or tasbi. The spelling depends on how Arabic sounds are represented in English and other languages. The terms generally refer to the same practice or the same prayer beads, depending on context.

Tasbih meaning in Islam: the idea behind SubhanAllah

The Arabic word tasbih is connected with declaring Allah free from every imperfection. The phrase most closely associated with tasbih is SubhanAllah, commonly translated as “Glory be to Allah.” When Muslims make tasbih, they remember Allah’s perfection and greatness. The word is also used more broadly in everyday English to describe a set of remembrance or the beads used to keep count.

Tasbih, tahmid, takbir and tahlil

These related terms describe different forms of dhikr. Tasbih is glorifying Allah, often by saying SubhanAllah. Tahmid is praising Allah with Alhamdulillah. Takbir is declaring Allah’s greatness with Allahu Akbar. Tahlil is the declaration La ilaha illallah. These phrases appear in many daily remembrance routines, but their counts and order should be taken from the specific narration or practice being followed.

What is a tasbih counter?

A tasbih counter is a tool that records repetitions as you say a phrase. A traditional misbaha or tasbeeh may use beads, while a digital tasbih counter uses a button, screen or small electronic device. An online tasbih counter works in a web browser and can be opened on a mobile phone, tablet or computer without installing a separate app.

How many beads are on a tasbeeh?

Prayer beads are made in different designs and counts. Sets of 33 beads and 99 beads are common, and some sets include a marker or separator. There is no need to assume every tasbeeh has the same number of beads. A person may also count on their fingers or use another suitable aid. For specific religious guidance about a preferred method, consult a knowledgeable scholar.

Common spellings: tasbih, tasbeeh, tasbi and tesbih

English spellings vary because Arabic sounds are represented in different ways. Tasbih and tasbeeh are both widely used; tasbeh, tasbi and tesbih also appear in searches and regional transliteration. These spelling differences do not necessarily indicate different practices. When learning the phrase itself, use a reliable Arabic text and listen to a trusted recitation rather than relying on an English spelling alone.

Frequently asked questions

What does tasbih mean in Islam?

It means glorifying Allah and declaring Him free from imperfection. SubhanAllah is a common phrase of tasbih.

Is tasbeeh the same as tasbih?

Yes. Tasbeeh is a common alternate English spelling of tasbih.

Do I need beads to count tasbih?

No. Prayer beads, fingers and digital counters can all help keep count. Choose a method that lets you focus on remembrance.
