Online tasbih counter features for everyday dhikr

This free online tasbih counter is designed for people who want a simple way to count tasbih, tasbeeh, zikr or dhikr on a phone or computer. The large digital counter makes each tap easy to see, while the progress ring gives a quick visual indication of how close you are to a chosen target. You can use the tool for a short set of 33 repetitions, a 99-count session, a 100-count personal target or a custom number that suits your routine.

Unlike a basic tally counter, the page includes common Arabic remembrance phrases with transliteration and a short meaning. Select SubhanAllah, Alhamdulillah, Allahu Akbar, La ilaha illallah, Astaghfirullah or another available dhikr. The counter is a counting aid: read and pronounce the words carefully, and keep your attention on the remembrance rather than on the screen.

28 ready-made dhikr in one counter

The selector groups 28 remembrances into four sets: core dhikr such as SubhanAllah, Alhamdulillah, Allahu Akbar and La ilaha illallah; istighfar phrases for seeking forgiveness; salawat on the Prophet ﷺ; and duas from the Quran and Sunnah. Each one shows the Arabic text, a transliteration and a short English meaning, and each keeps its own count, so switching phrases never erases your progress. You can also add your own dhikr with a name and target. The dhikr list page shows every phrase in one place.

Step size, sound and target alerts

Most dhikr is counted one recitation at a time, so the step size starts at 1. If you want to tally larger numbers, such as a set of 10 at once, choose a bigger step and each tap adds that amount. Guided routines always use steps of 1. With Sound turned on you hear a short tick for each tap and a three-note chime when you reach your target, so you can finish a set without watching the screen. On supported Android browsers a short vibration marks the target as well. Many iPhones and desktop browsers do not expose vibration, which is why the sound option exists.

Daily goal, streak and best day

Set a daily goal and the counter shows your progress toward it, your current day streak, your total and your best day. These figures are calculated on your device from your own taps. They are meant to support a steady habit, not to compare yourself with other people, and they disappear if you clear your browser data unless you export a backup first.

Count the 99 Names of Allah

The 99 Names study session opens a counter with no target so you can move through the Names at your own pace while reading them on the 99 Names of Allah page. The site does not prescribe a repetition count for the Names.

Digital tasbih, tasbeeh counter and zikr counter

People search for this kind of tool using different spellings, including digital tasbih counter, digital tasbeeh counter, online tasbeeh, online zikr counter, tesbih counter and dhikr counter online. These phrases often describe the same basic need: a convenient way to keep track of repeated remembrance. This site focuses on Islamic dhikr counting rather than general-purpose number tallying.

Use the counter with or without a target

Choose one of the preset goals or select the infinity option to count without a fixed finish point. If you are following a personal routine, the custom goal lets you enter another number. Undo corrects an accidental tap, while Reset starts the selected phrase again. Counts are stored locally in the browser on the device, and the export option lets you save a backup file. Because browser storage can be cleared, export a backup if you want to preserve your totals.

Does an online tasbih counter replace prayer beads?

No. A digital tasbih counter is simply another counting option. Some people count on their fingers, some use prayer beads (misbaha or tasbeeh), and others prefer a phone-based counter. The important point is to use a method that helps you keep track without distracting you from the words and their meaning. Follow the practice and counting method taught by the scholar or source you trust.

Privacy, offline use and device support

The counter does not require an account. Your counts and preferences are saved in local browser storage, not in a personal online profile. After the site has been opened once and its files have been cached, the counter may remain available offline on supported browsers. Vibration depends on device and browser support; many iPhones and some desktop browsers do not expose the web vibration feature, so the sound option can be used as an alternative where available.

Frequently asked questions

What is an online tasbih counter?

An online tasbih counter is a digital way to keep track of repeated dhikr. Tap once for each recitation instead of keeping the number in your head.

Are tasbih and tasbeeh the same?

They are common English spellings of the same Arabic word. People may also use “tasbih” to refer to prayer beads or a digital counter.

What is the difference between the after-salah and bedtime modes?

After salah, the guided routine is SubhanAllah 33, Alhamdulillah 33 and Allahu Akbar 34. Before sleep, the separate routine is Allahu Akbar 34, SubhanAllah 33 and Alhamdulillah 33.

Does the counter save my progress?

Yes. Counts, daily totals and settings are saved locally in your browser on your device. You can export a JSON backup from the counter.

Does it work offline?

After the first visit, the pages and counter are cached by your browser. You can then continue counting when you are offline, subject to your browser's storage settings.

Is this digital tasbih counter free?

Yes. The counter is free to use. The website may show ads to support its operation.

How many dhikr can I count with this tasbih counter?

The counter includes 28 ready-made dhikr in four groups: core dhikr, istighfar, salawat and duas from the Quran and Sunnah. Each has Arabic, transliteration and a short meaning, and you can add your own custom dhikr as well. See the full dhikr list.

Is it permissible to use a digital tasbih instead of counting on my fingers?

Many scholars treat prayer beads and counters as permissible aids for keeping count. Some prefer counting on the fingers, based on a narration reported in Sunan Abi Dawud and Jami at-Tirmidhi. This site is a counting tool, not a source of rulings, so ask a scholar you trust if you want a specific answer.

Do I need wudu to do dhikr?

Wudu is not a condition for making dhikr, although many people prefer to remember Allah in a state of purity. For rulings about other acts of worship, ask a qualified scholar.

What is the reward for saying SubhanAllah 100 times?

A narration in Sahih Muslim 2698 mentions that whoever glorifies Allah one hundred times has a thousand good deeds recorded or a thousand sins erased. Wording can differ between narrations and from other reports about SubhanAllahi wa bihamdihi, so read the source itself. See the SubhanAllah guide.

Can I count the 99 Names of Allah with this counter?

Yes. Open the 99 Names study session, which has no target, and tap once for each Name as you read it. See the 99 Names of Allah page for the list and meanings.

What does the step size do?

Step size sets how much each tap adds. It starts at 1, which suits normal dhikr. Larger steps help if you want to tally bigger numbers quickly. Guided routines always count in steps of 1, and Undo removes your last step.

Does the counter make a sound when I reach my target?

Yes, if Sound is on. You hear a short tick for each tap and a three-note chime when the target is reached. On supported Android browsers the device also vibrates. Many iPhones and desktop browsers do not support vibration.

How does the daily streak work?

The streak counts consecutive days on which you counted at least once. If you miss a day it starts again from 1. It is stored on your device only.

Why did my count disappear?

Counts are saved in your browser, so clearing site data, using private browsing or switching device or browser starts you from zero. Use Export backup regularly and Import backup to restore your counts on the same or another device.

How do I add the counter to my home screen?

On iPhone, open the site in Safari, tap Share and choose Add to Home Screen. On Android Chrome, open the menu and choose Add to Home screen or Install app. After the first visit it can keep working offline.

Can I use this counter for things other than dhikr?

It is built for dhikr, but you can create a custom counter with your own name and use the step size for a simple tally. Keep worship counts in their own dhikr so your daily totals stay meaningful.

Is the tasbih counter available in other languages?

Yes. The interface is available in العربية, اردو, Bahasa Indonesia and Türkçe. The guides are currently in English.
