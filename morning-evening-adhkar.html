Morning and Evening Adhkar: A Practical Daily Guide

A short introduction to morning adhkar (adhkar al-sabah) and evening adhkar, with selected remembrances, counts and references.

What are adhkar?

Adhkar are words of remembrance and supplication through which Muslims remember Allah. Morning and evening adhkar are recited at the beginning and end portions of the day. They include Qur’anic verses, short phrases of dhikr and longer supplications.

Many Muslims read morning adhkar after Fajr and before sunrise, and evening adhkar after Asr or later before night. The exact time window is discussed differently by scholars. This page is a short starter guide, not a complete replacement for a trusted adhkar book or qualified teacher.

Morning adhkar and evening adhkar: how to begin

Morning and evening adhkar are remembrances that Muslims read at the beginning and end portions of the day. Collections may include Qur'anic verses, short duas, declarations of faith and phrases of praise or seeking forgiveness. The exact list, wording and counts differ across collections, so use a recognised book or trusted teacher when building a full routine.

Adhkar al-sabah and adhkar al-masa

Adhkar al-sabah refers to morning remembrance, while adhkar al-masa refers to evening remembrance. Some supplications are specifically associated with morning or evening; others are general dhikr that may be said throughout the day. Read the heading and context in your chosen collection so that you know whether a particular dua is time-specific.

Examples of common daily remembrance

Common phrases include SubhanAllah, Alhamdulillah, Allahu Akbar, La ilaha illallah and Astaghfirullah. Some narrations mention SubhanAllahi wa bihamdihi with a specific count. This page gives a short introduction, not a complete or exhaustive morning and evening adhkar list. Check each phrase's full Arabic wording, translation and source before treating a count as prescribed.

How to make a consistent adhkar routine

  1. Choose a reliable adhkar collection with references.
  2. Start with a small portion that you can read carefully and consistently.
  3. Learn the Arabic text and meaning, using transliteration as a temporary aid.
  4. Keep prescribed counts separate from personal goals you set for practice.
  5. Review the routine with a knowledgeable teacher if you are unsure about a narration or ruling.

Can a digital dhikr counter help with morning and evening remembrance?

Yes, a digital counter can help track repetitions for a phrase when the source specifies a count. Frequently asked questions

Are morning and evening adhkar compulsory?

Muslims are encouraged to remember Allah. The status and wording of individual adhkar depend on their evidence; consult a trusted scholar or reliable hadith reference for detailed rulings.

Can I read adhkar from my phone?

Yes. A phone, book or printed list can help you read the words accurately. Make sure the source provides the full wording and reliable references.

Is this a complete morning and evening adhkar list?

No. This is a short selection to introduce the topic. Use a recognised adhkar collection for a complete routine.
