What is the tasbih before sleeping?

The bedtime remembrance often called the Tasbih of Fatimah is narrated in a report about Ali and Fatimah (may Allah be pleased with them). The Prophet ﷺ taught them a remembrance to say when they went to bed. The report says it would be better for them than having a servant.

One reported bedtime sequence: 34-33-33

OrderDhikrArabicCount
1Allahu Akbar (Takbir)اللَّٰهُ أَكْبَرُ34
2SubhanAllah (Tasbih)سُبْحَانَ اللَّٰهِ33
3Alhamdulillah (Tahmid)الْحَمْدُ لِلَّٰهِ33

Sources: Sahih Muslim 2727a and Sahih al-Bukhari 5362. These reports describe the bedtime remembrance with different ordering: one gives Takbir 34, Tasbih 33 and Tahmid 33; another gives Tasbih 33, Tahmid 33 and Takbir 34. The total is 100 in both. A related wording appears in Sahih al-Bukhari 6318. This is a bedtime practice; the post-salah remembrance is separately reported in Sahih Muslim 596a.

Bedtime dhikr and daily remembrance

This bedtime practice is one part of a wider routine of dhikr. You can also learn about morning and evening adhkar, or read the guide to dhikr after salah. Each practice has its own wording, count and context.

Bedtime dhikr: understanding the narrated wording

The remembrance commonly called Tasbih of Fatimah is associated with a teaching to Ali and Fatimah (may Allah be pleased with them). Reports describe saying Allahu Akbar, SubhanAllah and Alhamdulillah before sleeping, with the total adding up to 100. The order and presentation should be read carefully because narrations are not always worded in the same way.

Why do bedtime tasbih counts appear in different orders?

One report gives 34 takbir, 33 tasbih and 33 tahmid. Another familiar wording presents 33 SubhanAllah, 33 Alhamdulillah and 34 Allahu Akbar. Both add up to 100, but the sequence should be attributed to its own narration. The bedtime guide should not say that one order is the only valid version or imply that readers must confuse it with the post-salah routine.

What do the bedtime phrases mean?

SubhanAllah means “Glory be to Allah”; Alhamdulillah means “All praise is for Allah”; and Allahu Akbar means “Allah is the Greatest.” The phrases are short, but their meanings are central to Muslim remembrance. Take time to learn the Arabic wording and pronunciation rather than focusing only on reaching the final number.

When should the bedtime remembrance be read?

The narrations connect this remembrance with going to bed. A person can prepare for sleep, read the phrases in the order of the narration they are following and then complete the routine. Other bedtime supplications are also reported, so this short count should not be described as a complete list of everything to read before sleeping.

Frequently asked questions

How many times do I say Allahu Akbar before sleep?

In one version reported in Sahih Muslim 2727a, Allahu Akbar is said 34 times, followed by SubhanAllah 33 times and Alhamdulillah 33 times. Other reports present the wording/order differently.

What is Tasbih of Fatimah?

It is a common name for the remembrance taught to Ali and Fatimah. One reported wording gives 34 Takbir, 33 Tasbih and 33 Tahmid; consult the cited narrations for their variant wording.

Can I use a counter for this routine?

Yes. The bedtime mode on this site follows one reported order; other narrations give a different order.
